What you are agreeing to

  • You are giving your handwriting — the pen strokes you draw on this page — to this project to train handwriting recognition models.
  • The project is currently free and personal, but may become a commercial product. Your permission covers that, with no further approval needed and no payment to you.
  • Your samples are not published or resold. They are used to train and test models, and may be described in aggregate.
  • We store the strokes, the expression each one is a copy of, whether you used a finger, a pen or a mouse, and a random reference code. We do not ask for or store your name, email address or account. The service that receives your submission can technically see your IP address while handling it; we do not keep it with your samples.
  • Do not write anything personal. Copy only the printed expressions.
  • Taking part is voluntary. To have your samples deleted, email inkmath-project@proton.me with the reference code shown after you submit. Samples already used in a trained model cannot be taken back out of that model, but they will not be used again.

How to do it — about 10 minutes

  1. Use a finger or stylus on a phone or tablet if you can. A mouse or trackpad works, but is less useful to us.
  2. Copy the line shown exactly as printed, symbol for symbol. You do not need to understand the mathematics — several are deliberately nonsense.
  3. Write naturally, at your normal size and speed. Please do not tidy your handwriting. Ordinary handwriting is the whole point.
  4. Press Save & Next after each line. Press Submit when you want to finish — you can stop at any point, and partial progress is welcome.

Autosaves to this browser's local storage after every Save/Skip, so closing the tab or restarting is safe -- reopening this file resumes where you left off. "Download" exports everything captured so far (partial progress is fine to download and resume later).

Working across two machines? Local storage does not travel with the file. Download the JSON on the first machine, copy it across with this HTML file, then use "Import a downloaded JSON" here to carry on where you stopped. Importing MERGES: samples already captured on this machine are kept, and anything already done is skipped rather than asked again.
